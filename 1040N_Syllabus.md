### Assignment Overview
__Homework__ assignments will be assigned weekly. There will be 2 or 3 homework assignments that cover the material discussed throughout the week. Each lesson has an "Individual Preparation Assignment" and a "Homework Assignment". All assignments will be due by the end of the Saturday of the week assigned.

Throughout the semester, you will participate in multiple __projects__ to improve in your skills. These will be completed weekly and correspond with material studied through the week. The intent of these projects is to give a realistic scenario to apply what you have learned.

__Exams__ will be given periodically through the semester to give you opportunity to show your understanding. The __final exam__ will be given in the week after the end of classes as indicated on the course schedule.
* The midterm and final exams need to be scheduled with a proctor or a Snow College Testing Center at least one week ahead of the exam date. (For example, if the exam is on February 21, you should have a time scheduled by February 14.)

### Extra Credit Policy
As your instructor, my duty is to teach you and to achieve the outcomes outlined above. As such, I teach this course with a goal of understanding the material and satisfying those outcomes.

The concept of extra credit demonstrates that the goal is not to understand, but to just earn enough points to make it through. Extra credit undermines the goals of the course.

As such, *I will not award extra credit for the sake of getting more points*. If I see a need through the semester and as appropriate for the course, I will add extra assignments as needed, but with the express purpose of helping to understand difficult topics.

### Academic Integrity
You have paid me to learn everything you can. Academic dishonesty is thus a waste of the time and money you have committed to your education. Additionally, dishonest performance will *ruin* your future careers (assuming, of course, that you can get a career). I do not tolerate dishonesty in the classroom.

On the other hand, I know that there are incalculable resources to help you understand the class material. - Try homework and projects on your own as much as possible

    - Practice what you have learned (you can ask AI (in study mode) to create practice problems)
    - Complete the homework and projects
- When practicing for the exam, practice without assistance as much as possible
    - Use internet resources to learn about anything that causes confusion
    - Complete practice questions (from reviews or from AI in study mode) with as little assistance as possible
    - Continue practicing until you can solve problems without assistance

#### Using AI in this class
Generative AI is a technology that automates part or all of the writing and content creation process. Popular examples include ChatGPT (OpenAI), Gemini (Google), Claude (Anthropic), and others. Statistics is actually the core of Generative AI. Computers use statistics and probabilities to determine output text. 

Throughout the semester, you may only use generative AI when I provide specific direction to do so. We will use generative AI in a guided way to ensure that course learning outcomes and pedagogy are fully supported.

As the instructor, I reserve the right to disqualify homework, project, and exam scores from the grade if there is evidence of improper AI use. If improper AI use is suspected, I may call you in to discuss the situation. __Remember that you are in charge of your own education__. Do not cheat yourself just to make life easy. You won't have that chance in the professional world.

#### Consequences for cheating
Cheating for this class is considered to be using any resources to complete assignments or exams without actually understanding what is done. Examples of cheating include, but are not limited to,
- Copying another students' work
- Using AI to complete the assignment
- Bringing extra notes that aren't allowed into exams

Consequences for cheating are:
- Homework and Projects
    - Alerts and discussions with the professor
    - 0 for assignments if the problems persist
- Exams

    - There will be absolutely NO tolerance for cheating on the final exam. Cheating on the final exam will result in a failing grade for the course.
- The professor has the right to determine other consequences based on the circumstances

Your job in this class is to learn. The purpose of exams is to show me how much you have learned. Don't sacrifice your entire future by dishonesty. If you are concerned about upcoming exams, come talk to me and study.

## Course Description (optional)
This course will provide students with an introductory look at basic principles of statistics while emphasizing collaboration, critical thinking, and problem solving within practical contexts. Topics covered will include: designing studies, organizing data, finding measures center and variation, computing probability, using discrete and continuous probability distributions, estimating sample size, hypothesis testing, finding confidence intervals for one and two populations, and running a correlation and regression analysis.

## Course Student Learning Outcomes
The __Introduction to Statistics__ course is a General Education course and satisfies the Quantitative Literacy requirement for graduation. As such, you can expect to learn the following:

__General Education Outcomes__:
1. Fundamental knowledge of human cultures and the natural world
2. How to read and research in the physics discipline
3. How to draw from other subjects and answer questions using physics
4. Thinking analytically, critically, and creatively
5. Communication through writing and speaking
6. Reasoning mathematically

__Quantitative Literacy Outcomes__:
1. Interpret various graphs and charts
2. Convert information into mathematical forms (such as equations, graphs, tables, etc.)
3. Complete basic calculations to solve problems
4. Problem solve across multiple disciplines using quantitative reasoning and quantitative literacy
5. Use evidence to support a claim

__Student Learning Outcomes__:
1. Understand and apply elements of quality study design.
2. Be familiar with many common graphs and charts and will be able to create an appropriate graph or chart for a given data set.
3. Understand the meaning of statistical measures (mean, median, proportion, standard deviation) and be able to calculate each of them for a given data set.
4. Be able to take a given problem and, as appropriate, complete a hypothesis test or compute a confidence interval.
5. Be able to make an appropriate real world conclusion based on the results from the hypothesis test or confidence interval.
