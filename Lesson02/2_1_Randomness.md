## Lesson
In the lesson 1, we looked at the nature of data and variables. Now, we turn to collecting the data.

Ideally, we want to collect ALL information so we can see the entire picture.
- A __Population__ is the entire group in a study
    - If you want to know the quality of a certain car model, its population would be ALL cars of that model that have been made (even if you miss one, it is no longer a population)
- A __Parameter__ is a measurement of a variable for a population

As you can imagine, measuring the parameter of a population could be extremely difficult. It would take a lot of time, money, and other resources to gather that data. In fact, the closest we ever get would be a census or an election. Even then, we don't get everyone, so it's an imperfect measurement.

So, how do we solve this? This is the point of statistics - we gather a small amount of data (called a sample) and use statistical mathematics to infer information about the population. For example, we count the election ballots for a state, then using statistics, we can determine the probability of a certain result.
- A __Sample__ is a subset of a Population
- A __Statistic__ is a measurement of a variable for a sample
In order for a sample to be a proper subset of the population, 2 basic requirements have to be met:

1. A sample has to be __random__ (meaning everyone/everything in a population has an equal chance of being selected)
2. A sample has to appropriately represent the population (All groups within the population are represented in the sample)

Any sample that isn't random or that doesn't represent all groups within the population can produce results that lean more towards certain groups than to other groups. This error in results is known as bias. Any sample that has __bias__ (not random or doesn't represent all groups) cannot be used to infer information about the population.
