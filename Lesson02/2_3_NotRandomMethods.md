# Lesson 2.3 Not-so-random Sampling Methods
## Reading
Reading selections are from the [Introductory Statistics Textbook](../Resources/OpenIntroTextbook.pdf)
* 1.4.2 Sampling methods, non-random sampling methods, and Bias (pages 22-25)

## Lesson
In the last lesson, we looked at some random sampling methods. There are other ways to sample a population that are commonly used which are not very random. Here are two not-so-random sampling methods:

| Sampling Method | How it works | Example | Problems with this method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Volunteer Response Sampling | Make survey available to all then ask volunteers to answer survey | Putting up a table in the plaza and offer cookies to anyone who completes the survey | - Bias toward people who like cookies<br>- Excludes anyone with food allergies<br>- Excludes anyone in a hurry that doesn't have time to stop |
| Convenience Sampling | Asking people who are available to answer survey | To gather data for a class, you ask your roommates and other students on your floor | - Bias toward people you hang out with <br>- Excludes anyone who doesn't spend time with you |
