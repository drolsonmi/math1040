# Lesson 3.3 Blind Studies
## Reading
Reading sections are from the [Introductory Statistics Textbook](../Resources/OpenIntroTextbook.pdf)

## Lesson
In section [3.2 Experimental Studies](./3_2_ExperimentalStudies.md), we learned how to design an experimental study. However, there is a potential problem. Could knowing whether you are in a control or treatment group affect the results? For example, if we are testing a new vaccine, would knowing you got the vaccine affect your reaction to it? Could getting the injection make you feel sick, or could not getting the injection make you think you're catching the disease?

In such cases, it might be good to hide which group you are in. A study where the sample doesn't know which group they are in is known as a __blind study__. In order to create a blind study, members of the control group need something to hide the fact that they are in the control group. For this, we often give them a fake treatment. The fake treatment is known as a __placebo__.

Occasionally, we also want to avoid bias from the researchers knowing which group the subjects belong to. A study designed where neither the subject nor the researcher know what groups the samples belong to is known as a __double blind study__.

### Summary
* __Blind Study__: When researchers keep the subjects uninformed about whether they are in the Control Group or the Treatment Group
* __Double Blind Study__: When neither the researchers nor the subjects are informed about whether the subjects are in the Control Group or the Treatment Group
* __Placebo__: A fake treatment to keep subjects from knowing which group they are in
