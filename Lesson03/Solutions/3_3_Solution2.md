## 3.3 Blind Studies - Solution for Practice 2
An agricultural researcher wants to know if a new fertilizer increases corn yield. They randomly assign 30 plots of farmland to two groups. The treatment group receives the new fertilizer; the control group receives plain water applied in the exact same amount, at the same times, by the same workers. The workers applying the treatments know which plots receive fertilizer and which receive water. At harvest, an independent lab measures the bushels of corn per acre without being told which plots received which treatment.

Identify the placebo, explain what type of blind study this is, and discuss any remaining sources of bias.

## Solution
There are many ways to establish a study that will answer this question. Below is one possible solution.
- __Placebo__: The plain water applied to the control group plots in the same quantity and schedule as the fertilizer.
- __Type of Blind Study__: This is a single-blind study — the lab measuring the harvest (the "assessors") does not know which plots received the fertilizer, but the workers applying the treatments do know.
