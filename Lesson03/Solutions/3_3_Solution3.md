## 3.3 Blind Studies - Solution for Practice 3
A school district wants to test whether a new reading curriculum improves student test scores. They select 6 elementary schools and randomly assign 3 to use the new curriculum and 3 to continue with the existing curriculum. Teachers in all schools are told only that the district is "evaluating instructional materials" — they are not told which group is the treatment group and which is the control. At the end of the year, student reading tests are graded by an outside scoring company that has no information about which schools used which curriculum.

Identify whether a placebo is present, explain what type of blind study this is, and evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of the design.

## Solution
There are many ways to establish a study that will answer this question. Below is one possible solution.
- __Placebo__: There is no traditional placebo here, but the existing curriculum serves as the control condition. Because both groups are actively teaching reading (just with different materials), students in the control group are still receiving instruction — they are not left with nothing. This parallel structure limits the possibility that any improvement in the treatment group is simply due to doing something new rather than the curriculum itself.
- __Type of Blind Study__: This is a double-blind study — the teachers do not know whether their school is in the treatment or control group, and the outside scorers do not know which schools used which curriculum.
