## Lesson 7 Measures of Spread
In [lesson 6](../Lesson06/index.html), we learned about how to locate the center of our data. We looked at the mean, median, and mode. We also saw how to calculate the 5-number summary and create a boxplot to get a view of the distribution. In lesson 7, we will get a closer look at the the distribution by looking at the spread of the data.

The basic measurement for the spread of data is the __standard deviation__. Large standard deviations indicate that the data is spread out. Small standard deviations indicate that the data is clustered close to the mean.

After we learn about the standard deviation, we will see a few applications on how to read and use the standard deviation in a normal distribution. We'll learn about the __z-score__ and the __empirical rule__.

## Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to accomplish the following:

- Calculate and interpret the variance and standard deviation
- Be able to calculate the z-score and describe its meaning
- Use the Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7 Rule) to estimate the percentage of data falling within 1, 2, and 3 standard deviations of the mean for a bell-shaped distribution
- Use z-scores and the Empirical Rule together to identify unusual or outlier values in a dataset
