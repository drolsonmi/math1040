# Lesson 10: Compound Probabilities
In Lesson 9, we calculated the probability of a single event. But most interesting questions in the real world involve more than one event at a time: What's the probability a patient tests positive **or** has the disease? What's the probability someone owns a car **and** has a part-time job? What's the probability of drawing two aces in a row from a deck of cards? These are all examples of **compound probabilities** — probabilities that combine two or more events using "OR," "AND," and conditional relationships.
 
In this lesson, we'll learn how to combine probabilities correctly, depending on whether events overlap, whether one event affects another, and whether events happen at the same time or in sequence. By the end, you'll have all the tools needed to calculate the probability of almost any combination of events.
 
## Lesson Objectives
 
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to accomplish the following:
 
- Calculate "OR" probabilities using the general addition rule, and simplify the rule for mutually exclusive events
- Determine whether two events are mutually exclusive
- Calculate and interpret a conditional probability
- Calculate "AND" probabilities using the general multiplication rule
- Determine whether two events are independent, and simplify the multiplication rule when they are
