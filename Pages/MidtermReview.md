## How to study
Here are a few tips on how to study:
* Review past homework assignments and exams
    - Any questions that you struggled on indicate topics that need further study
        - For each homework/exam question (even if you got it right), ask yourself, "Can I explain why I got this right/wrong?" 
        - If you can't explain, this is a topic that you may want to study more.
* Practice quizzes
    - There are practice quizzes for each lesson
    - Go through the practice quiz questions
        - Any questions you can't answer (or can't explain why you got it right/wrong) indicate topics that need further study

## Using AI
If you want more questions, create a prompt for AI to generate practice questions. 

- Here is an example prompt for a practice question:

    > "I am preparing for an exam. I need a practice question related to (topic in question). Do not provide the answer or any tips."

- If you get stuck, you can get helpful hints. Example prompts for hints:

    > - "I am struggling to answer this question. Could you help me understand why this is related to (topic in question)?"
    > - "I am struggling to answer this question. Can you show me where to start? Don't give me the answer, though."
    > - "I was able to find {last part done on your own}. I'm not too sure where to go from here. Could you give me ONLY the next step?"

- When you complete the practice problem, you can check your answer. Example prompts for revealing the answer:

    > - "I got {answer}. Is this correct?"
    > - "Could you show me the answer now with all the details?"

If the answer provided by AI is different, you can prompt AI to describe its answer or to teach you more about the topic. 
- *Remember that the answer provided by AI could be incorrect!* I like to take the problem and put it into another AI provider and see if they get the same answer. If the answers are different, I put the problem into a 3rd AI provider.

### Segment 3: Categorical Analysis

To measure a categorical variable, we have to create a numerical representation of the data. So, we introduced the concept of probability:

- Probability
    - Relative Frequency and the Law of Large Numbers
- Compound Probability
    - $P(A~and~B) = P(A)\cdot P(B|A)$
        - $P(B|A)$ is a conditional probability: The probability that B happens given that we know A has already happened
    - $P(A~or~B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A~and~B)$
- Rules of Counting
    - Fundamental Counting Rule
    - Factorials
    - Permutations (${}_nP_r$) and Combinations (${}_nC_r$)
- Binomial Distributions
    - Probability that in a sample you have exactly r successes:  $P(X=r)={}_nC_r p^r (1-p)^{n-r}$ or `binompdf(` on a TI-83/84
    - Probability that in a sample you have up to r successes:  $P(X\le r) = P(X=0) + P(X=1) + \dots + P(X=r)$ or `binomcdf(` on a TI-83/84
